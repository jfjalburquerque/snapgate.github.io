In one line

Snapgate asks you, right from the notification, whether each new photo should back up to Google Photos.

In a paragraph

Google Photos backs up by folder, and everything from the camera lands in the same one. So receipts, parking spots, screenshots and meeting whiteboards end up in the cloud next to the photos that matter, eating into the free 15 GB. Snapgate adds what's missing: a decision per photo. Seconds after you take a photo, a notification shows a thumbnail with two buttons, Upload and Discard. Discarded photos stay on the phone untouched. Temporary modes and rules by time, day or folder handle the moments when you don't want to be asked. There are no servers, no ads and no analytics, and the Google permission it uses can only add photos, never read or delete them.
