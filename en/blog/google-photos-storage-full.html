Google Photos storage full? How to free up space without losing photos

5 November 2026 · jfjalburquerque

Google's free 15 GB aren't just for photos: Google Photos, Gmail and Google Drive share them. When they fill up, photo backup stops, and Gmail may stop receiving email. Before paying for more storage, it's worth seeing what's actually using it.

1. See what's taking the space

In Google Photos, tap your profile picture and go to Photos settings → Manage storage (or open one.google.com/storage). You'll see how much each service uses and, in Photos, a tool that groups what's usually safe to delete: blurry photos, screenshots and large videos.

2. Start with large videos

One minute of 4K video takes as much space as hundreds of photos. Going through the large videos list and deleting three or four you don't need often frees several gigabytes at once — far more than deleting photos one by one.

3. Screenshots and photos that were only for a moment

Screenshots, receipts, where you parked, a meeting whiteboard. Search for "screenshots" or "receipts" in Google Photos: it groups them for you. Delete them and empty the trash, or they keep counting for 60 days.

4. Check your backup quality

Under Photos settings → Backup → Backup quality you can pick Storage saver instead of Original quality. Photos are slightly compressed and videos reduced to 1080p; for phone photos the difference is barely visible. Note that it only applies to what you upload from now on.

5. Keep it from filling up again

Freeing space works, but if everything keeps uploading on its own, you'll be back here in a few months. The underlying problem is that Google Photos backup is all or nothing: it uploads every camera photo, including the ones you only needed for ten minutes.
